Introduction

Tomorrow, companies will not pay for a day rate. They will pay for your system. I am betting everything on the system: mine has been doing 80% of my SEO for months. The remaining 20% is me (strategy, proprietary data, judgment). And that 20% is exactly what gets invoiced. This guide takes you from 0 to 1: by the end, you have your agent, and you run it on your own.

To get there, you make two moves. The first: from chatbot to agent. With a chatbot, you ask a question, it answers, it forgets. Next conversation, you explain everything again. An agent is the same Claude, but with your memory, your processes and your data: it runs automatically at fixed times and it learns from what it publishes. A junior consultant available around the clock: you steer, it executes.

The second move: from Claude Cowork to the terminal plus Obsidian. Cowork is Claude's chat window, the one you already use. The terminal is the same intelligence, but acting on your computer: it reads your files, writes them, runs your skills. And Obsidian is the free application you use to read and organize those files. The terminal looks a little scary at first, but it is extremely simple: you are just changing chat window. Chapter 2 makes the move in 15 minutes.

This is the logical follow-up to my first guide (the SEO system on Claude, the 9 skills). The first one laid down the skills. This one lays down everything around them: memory, learning loops, automated routines. That is the difference between a Claude that executes well and an agent that improves from one mission to the next.

Before you start

A few definitions before you start, because nobody is born knowing what a vault is:

  • Chatbot : Claude's chat window. You ask a question, it answers, it forgets.
  • Agent : Claude + your memory + your skills + routines + loops. It acts on your files and improves from one mission to the next.
  • Cowork : Claude's desktop application. Still the chatbot, better equipped.
  • Terminal : your computer's command window. That is where Claude Code runs, the version of Claude that acts instead of only answering.
  • Obsidian : a free application to read and organize notes stored as text files.
  • Vault : Obsidian's word for your notes folder. A plain folder of text files on your computer: it is your agent's memory.
  • Skill : your method encoded in a file, written once, executable forever.
  • Routine : a task that starts on its own, at a fixed time, automatically.
  • Loop : what gets published comes back measured, and the measurement corrects the next decisions. That is what makes the system learn.
  • Autonomy step : the share of the work that happens automatically. A skill runs when you ask for it, a routine starts on its own. From skill to routine, you step in less and less.

Chapter 1

From assistant to agent

An assistant answers and forgets. An agent remembers, runs your method, works automatically and corrects itself with your real results. The difference comes down to 4 components.

The 4 components

When you open Claude and ask a question, you are using an assistant. It is good, but it starts from zero at every conversation. You re-explain your client, your sector, your rules. The result depends on the prompt you happened to write that day.

An agent is the same Claude, plus four things you build around it:

The 4 components of an agent
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 1. MEMORY         a vault of markdown files that the
        |          agent re-reads at every session
        ▼
 2. SKILLS         your method encoded, versioned,
        |          executable forever
        ▼
 3. ROUTINES       tasks that start on their own,
        |          at fixed times, automatically
        ▼
 4. LOOPS          what gets published comes back
                   measured, and corrects the decisions
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 Assistant = Claude alone.  Agent = Claude + these 4 components.

The 4 components, in the order you build them. Each chapter of this guide lays down one.

And so you do not mix everything up: skill, workflow, loop, routine are not the same thing. Each one is an autonomy step, meaning the share of the work that happens automatically. A skill runs when you ask for it. A routine starts on its own, at a fixed time. Workflow and loop sit in between:

From skill to routine
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 SKILL      one isolated ability. Does ONE precise thing
            (the quick wins, internal linking…)      ch. 5
     ↓
 WORKFLOW   skills chained together. Delivers a
            complete result (the 8-phase audit…)     ch. 7
     ↓
 LOOP       a closed circuit: what comes out goes
            back to correct what goes in. The system
            no longer only produces, it LEARNS        ch. 8
     ↓
 ROUTINE    what starts all of it at a fixed time,
            automatically                             ch. 9
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 A skill executes. A workflow delivers. A loop learns.
 A routine runs. The agent is the four together.

The classic mistake: stopping at skills and thinking you have an agent. Without loops, the system produces but does not learn. Without routines, it only exists when you open it.

Why build one

An SEO tool sells you commodity work: keyword volumes everyone has, scores everyone looks at. Nobody pays for commodity work. And tomorrow the bare skill will be commonplace: players in the market already sell prefabricated SEO skills, the value of the skill alone tends toward zero.

What stays worth paying for is the context you inject (your proprietary data) and the human arbitration on the way out. The agent is what turns those two things into production at scale. It cannot be copied, because its raw material is you.

The trap to avoid right now. An SEO agent is not Claude on its own. If you are not good at SEO, Claude will not be good. The agent amplifies your method, it does not replace it. This whole guide rests on that position: you stay the manager, the agent executes.

What you need

Claude Code (Anthropic's command line tool, where the agent can read and write files), a Claude subscription (Pro is enough to start), git installed, and several sessions ahead of you. You do not build an agent in one afternoon. You build it component by component, producing from the first one onward (and that is a good thing: a perfect system that has never produced a single page is worth nothing).

Chapter 2

From Claude Cowork to Obsidian

The first guide ran on Claude Cowork. The agent works in a folder on your computer, opened by two windows: Obsidian for you, Claude Code for it.

Cowork, the right starting point

If you followed the first guide, you work in Claude Cowork: your local working folder, your 9 skills dropped in. That was the right call to get started: zero technical setup, the context persists, the skills trigger on their own when your request matches.

Skill seo-quick-win activated. I am starting from your export and producing the quick wins table: the pages close to the top 3 whose click-through rate is not keeping up…

Claude Cowork: your skills on the left, the conversation on the right. That is assistant mode, and it works very well.

Why the agent moves local

Except that an agent needs things Cowork cannot do. It reads and writes dozens of files per session, and your vault will hold hundreds: Cowork is not built to handle that many at once. Nothing runs there at a fixed time either, whereas routines are the whole point of an agent (we set them up in chapter 9). And going local brings two more things: git records every change, so you can roll back anything, always. And Obsidian shows you your notes and the links between them, so you see what your agent knows instead of guessing.

The same folder, two windows

The move fits in one sentence: your Cowork working folder becomes your vault, and you open it with two tools at the same time. Obsidian is your window: you read, you navigate, you correct. Claude Code is its window: it reads, it writes, it runs the skills, it automates. No synchronization to manage, it is the same folder on your disk.

The move, step by step (15 minutes)

This is the move I walk people through in the bootcamp, screen by screen. Follow the steps in order, and at each step I show you what you should see: your screen shows the same thing, you carry on. You do not touch Cowork.

1 · Create your working folder in Documents

Open your file explorer (Mac: Finder · Windows: File Explorer). Go to Documents. Right click → "New folder" → name it my-vault, with no space and no accent. Everything in this guide gets built inside that folder. If you already have a working folder from the first guide, do not create a new one: move yours into Documents, and that becomes your vault.

One rule only about the location: the folder stays in Documents, on your disk. Not in iCloud, Google Drive or OneDrive: synchronization breaks file reading when Claude opens several at once.

A case seen for real. A folder in Google Drive that shows up empty while the files are right there: that is "Stream" mode, which presents a virtual disk the terminal cannot read. Switch Google Drive to "Mirror" mode, or simpler: keep your folder in Documents.

2 · Install Obsidian (to SEE your notes)

Download it for free at obsidian.md. Open it → "Open folder as vault" → pick your working folder. That is it: you now see your files and the links between them.

3 · Install Node.js (needed for what follows)

Go to nodejs.org, download the LTS version, install it (you click "Next" all the way). To check, open your Terminal (Mac: Spotlight → "Terminal" · Windows: Start menu → "PowerShell") and type:

Terminal · you
$ node --version
v20.11.1
# a number shows up = you are good

4 · Install Claude Code (to DRIVE Claude)

In the same Terminal, paste this line, then Enter:

Terminal · you
$ npm install -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code

added 248 packages in 12s
# the exact number varies, it does not matter: no error = you are good

5 · Put your skills in the right place

The Terminal always reads your skills from one precise folder: .claude/skills at the root of your user profile. Cowork stores its own somewhere else, and the two do not talk to each other: that is THE classic blocker. The clean fix is a shortcut (symbolic link) so both look at the same folder. You add a skill once, both see it:

# Mac · if a .claude/skills folder already exists, we move it aside
mv ~/.claude/skills ~/.claude/skills-old 2>/dev/null

# then we create the link (replace with the path to YOUR skills folder)
ln -s ~/Documents/Cowork/Skills ~/.claude/skills

These commands print no message when they succeed. No message = you are good. Then you check:

Terminal · you
$ ls ~/.claude/skills
seo-recherche-mots-cles     seo-clustering-mots-cles
seo-brief-contenu           maillage-systeme
seo-mots-cles-decisionnels  indexation-check   …
# your skill folders show up = you are good

On Windows: PowerShell as administrator, then New-Item -ItemType SymbolicLink. If it refuses, turn on Developer Mode in Settings and try again.

6 · Create the CLAUDE.md file (the manual Claude reads on its own)

In Obsidian, create a note named CLAUDE at the root of the folder (Obsidian adds the .md on its own), and paste your doctrine into it. That is the AGENTS.md file from chapter 3: the complete starting version is there, ready to copy. Check that it sits at the root, not in a subfolder.

7 · Launch and check

Move into your folder, launch Claude, and type /skills :

Terminal · my-vault
$ cd ~/Documents/my-vault
$ claude

  ✳ Claude Code · folder: my-vault

> /skills

Available skills :
  seo-recherche-mots-cles
  seo-clustering-mots-cles
  seo-brief-contenu
  maillage-systeme
  …

You see your list of skills: you are operational. Empty list? Redo step 5, the folder must be named exactly skills inside .claude.

8 · To start a session again later

At every new Terminal window, you simply retype the same two lines:

Terminal · you
$ cd ~/Documents/my-vault
$ claude
How does it work? You have nothing to explain to Claude. As soon as you run claude in your folder, it reads CLAUDE.md on its own, the manual for that folder, and it already knows how to work in there. You never have to paste your context again.
Chapter 3

The foundation: the vault and the doctrine

Your agent's memory is a folder of text files versioned in git. Two layers, one golden rule, and a doctrine file read at every session.

Why markdown files

Not a database, not one more SaaS. Text files you can read yourself in any editor (Obsidian if you want to navigate them comfortably), versioned in git so you never lose anything. Claude Code reads and writes these files natively. That is the whole difference with chat: the agent reads what is there, without you having to paste every time.

The structure: two layers

my-vault/
├── AGENTS.md              → your doctrine (read at every session)
├── raw/                   → raw sources, IMMUTABLE
│   ├── articles/          → industry watch, clippings
│   ├── data/              → Search Console exports, crawls
│   ├── clients/           → client notes, briefs received
│   └── notes/             → your notes, your call transcripts
└── wiki/                  → what the agent digests and structures
    ├── index.md           → the catalog of every page
    ├── log.md             → the journal, append only
    ├── hypotheses.md      → convictions not proven yet
    ├── contradictions.md  → pages that contradict each other
    ├── ingest-backlog.md  → the digestion backlog, sorted
    ├── sources/           → one sheet per ingested source
    ├── concepts/          → your SEO concepts (one file each)
    ├── briefs/            → the briefs produced
    └── evidence/          → published content ↔ measured result

The complete vault structure. The registers (hypotheses, contradictions, ingest-backlog, evidence) serve the loops in chapter 8: create them empty right now.

Non-negotiable rule. raw/ is read-only. The agent draws from it, it never touches it. Everything it produces goes into wiki/. That is the separation between your raw material and processed knowledge, and it is what keeps you from polluting your sources.

The doctrine: the AGENTS.md file

At the root, a file Claude reads at the start of every session. It is what keeps the agent from falling back into the average corpus. Here is mine, in its starting version, ready to adapt:

# AGENTS.md · doctrine of the SEO agent

You read this file at the start of EVERY session, then you apply it.

## Mission
Build and maintain an SEO system oriented toward conversion.
We are after the qualified lead, not traffic.

## SEO doctrine (applies to EVERYTHING you produce)
- Conversion, not visibility. A page exists to collect a
  qualified email (quote, calculator, meeting).
- Business keywords only: decision-stage and transactional.
  Informational queries are already eaten by the AI engines.
- Originality, not copying. We give the information the
  others do not give.
- Volume is not a relevant selection criterion.

## Hard rules
1. ZERO invented numbers. No source = no claim.
   A missing figure is written "to be sourced", never a
   plausible number.
2. raw/ is immutable. Read-only, always.
3. Every output goes into a file in wiki/, never as a volatile
   answer in the chat.
4. Every page in wiki/ cites its sources and points to at
   least 2 other pages in the vault.
5. Every action is recorded in wiki/log.md:
   ## [YYYY-MM-DD] action | short title
6. If a new source contradicts an existing page:
   do not overwrite. Record the conflict in wiki/contradictions.md.
7. Strict anti-AI writing on everything written
   (see raw/notes/my-voice.md and the checklist).

## What you never decide alone
- Publishing content (quality control must pass AND I approve).
- Changing the doctrine or a skill.
- Settling a hypothesis (that goes through an evidence sheet, real data).

Your doctrine v1. Deliberately short: every rule in there gets applied, every filler rule gets ignored. It will grow with your corrections (chapter 9).

What you have at the end of this chapter: an agent that knows your rules before writing a single line. Initialize git (git init, one commit per working session) and move on to chapter 4.

Chapter 4

Proprietary data

Without proprietary data going in, the agent hands you Claude's average corpus. So, commodity work. Data is what makes your output original and hard to copy.

What you collect

SourceWhat it gives the agent
Sales calls (transcribed)The exact vocabulary of your prospects, their real objections, the questions they ask before buying
Support tickets, client emailsThe real friction points, phrased in the real words
Client reviewsWhat they liked, said the way they say it
Search ConsoleYour real queries, the ones you already show up on without knowing it
Past engagements (audits, briefs)Everything you have already learned once, reusable

You anonymize whatever needs it before dropping anything into raw/. And if you have nothing usable, you do not produce: you start by going to get the data. That is the prerequisite, not an option. I have been saying it for a long time: you are no longer SEOs who create pages, you are SEOs who collect data.

The ingestion workflow

Dropping a file in is not enough. The agent has to digest it, and digestion always follows the same path (you can encode it as a skill, see chapter 5):

Ingesting a source · the mandatory path
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 raw/notes/prospect-call-X.md       (the source, immutable)
        |
        |  1. READ the file in full
        ▼
 wiki/sources/2026-06-call-X.md     2. STRUCTURED SHEET:
        |                              context, key figures,
        |                              limits, SEO implications
        ▼
 wiki/concepts/*.md                 3. CONNECT: update the
        |                              concepts touched + flag
        |                              any contradiction
        ▼
 wiki/log.md                        4. LOG one line
        |
        ▼
 inline summary                     5. REPORT: 5 bullets +
                                       "which SEO angle to dig?"

One source at a time. It is slow at the start, and that is the point: every digested source enriches the network, and the network makes the next outputs better.

Finding information again

At the start, plain text search is enough: the agent searches the vault by simple word matching. Once the vault goes past a few hundred files, you add a local semantic search index (free, it runs on your own computer, nothing to pay). The idea matters more than the tool: the agent has to answer "what do we already know about X" before producing anything about X. That is what makes your hundredth brief better than your first.

The real subject. Undigested data is data that does not exist for the agent. Your raw/ folder will always fill up faster than it gets digested, that is normal and it is dangerous. The loop that makes this backlog visible comes in chapter 8.

Where your data goes (privacy and GDPR)

The question always comes up, so you may as well answer it before someone asks. Claude Code sends your requests to Anthropic's servers: everything the agent reads in order to work passes through them for the duration of the processing. What matters is what Anthropic is allowed to do with it, and that depends on your account type.

On a business account (API, Team, Enterprise), your data is never used to train the models: that is the default setting of the commercial terms, nothing to configure. On a personal account (Free, Pro, Max), since the summer of 2025 there is a "Help improve Claude" setting in claude.ai → Settings → Privacy. If it is on, your conversations and coding sessions are used for training and kept for 5 years. Turn it off: no more training, 30-day retention. That is the first thing to check before putting client data into your vault.

And do not confuse training with GDPR, those are two subjects. If you handle client data (recorded calls, emails, support tickets), three reflexes: tell participants when you record a call, that is the baseline and it comes before any tool; prefer a business account, because Anthropic then provides a data processing agreement (the DPA) that frames the processing legally; and keep in the vault only what serves a purpose, anonymized whenever possible.

The big advantage of the vault. The raw material stays with you, locally, in your files. Nothing sits in a cloud tool. You only send Anthropic what serves the processing at hand, and on a business account that never trains their models.
Chapter 5

The agent's skills

A skill is your method encoded once, versioned, executed identically every time. The difference with a prompt: a prompt drifts, a skill improves.

Anatomy of an agent skill

A folder inside ~/.claude/skills/, with a SKILL.md file. Claude loads it automatically when your request matches its description. Each skill holds seven sections:

SKILL.md · anatomy of the file
─────────────────────────────────────────────

 ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
 │ ---                             │   file header
 │ name: my-skill                  │   · name = folder name
 │ description: |                  │   · description = what
 │   What the skill does…          │     triggers the skill
 │   ALWAYS use when: …            │     (Claude reads it to
 │ ---                             │     decide to activate)
 ├─────────────────────────────────┤
 │ # Skill · My Skill              │
 │ ## When to trigger              │   markdown body
 │ ## Required input               │   · 7 standard sections
 │ ## Pipeline (N steps)           │   · the "how",
 │ ## Mandatory output             │     step by step
 │ ## Absolute rules               │
 │ ## Related concepts             │
 └─────────────────────────────────┘

The anatomy of a skill. Two YAML fields for automatic triggering, markdown sections for the procedure. The "Absolute rules" section is the one that protects your credibility.

A complete SKILL.md, ready to copy

The ingestion skill, the one that runs the workflow from chapter 4. Create the folder ~/.claude/skills/ingest-source/ and paste this into SKILL.md :

---
name: ingest-source
description: |
  Ingesting a source into the vault: reads a file from raw/,
  creates the structured sheet in wiki/sources/, updates the
  concepts touched, logs it in wiki/log.md.

  ALWAYS use when the user says: "ingest",
  "digest this file", "add this source to the vault",
  "process raw/...".
---

# Skill · Ingesting a source

## When to trigger
A file in raw/ to digest. One source at a time, never several
at once without an explicit request.

## Required input
| Source | Required |
|--------|----------|
| Path of the file in raw/ | Yes |
| Reading angle wanted     | Recommended |

## Pipeline (5 steps)
1. Read the file IN FULL. No skimming.
2. Create wiki/sources/YYYY-MM-DD-short-name.md:
   context, method, key figures (sourced), limits,
   SEO implications. Short exact quotes only.
3. For each concept touched: if the wiki/concepts/ page
   exists, enrich it; otherwise create it with 2 outbound
   links. If the source CONTRADICTS an existing page: do not
   overwrite, record the conflict in wiki/contradictions.md.
4. Update wiki/index.md.
5. Append to wiki/log.md:
   ## [YYYY-MM-DD] ingest | title of the source

## Mandatory output
The source sheet + an inline summary in 5 bullets ending with:
"which SEO angle do you want to dig into?"

## Absolute rules
- raw/ stays intact. Read-only.
- No figure reused without its source.
- One source at a time.

## Related concepts
proprietary-data · ingest-backlog · contradictions

The first system skill of your agent. The SEO skills (research, clustering, brief…) follow exactly the same anatomy.

Every new skill follows the same anatomy, and you add it when the need shows up, never before.

The install order

You do not need everything on day one. The order that works: ingest-source first (to feed the vault), then seo-quick-win, the skill I run first as soon as a client gives me access to their Search Console: before thinking about creating new content, you clear the backlog of opportunities already there. You run it on a real project, and you add the others when the need shows up.

My rule. A badly written skill produces bad work at scale. You build the skills one at a time, carefully, and you correct them every time an output disappoints you. Today's correction is tomorrow's rule (chapter 10).
Chapter 6

The voice

The agent takes on your expressions and your tone of voice. Two files are enough.

The corpus: my-voice.md

A file (or a folder) in raw/notes/ holding your own texts: the posts that worked, your emails, passages from your newsletter. Plus your rules: what you say, what you never say, your expressions. The agent reads it before every piece of writing and copies the style. You can let Claude generate a first version from your existing content: you open it, you correct, you approve.

The anti-AI-writing checklist

A control list the agent runs on every text before handing it to you. To paste into raw/notes/anti-ia-writing.md and reference in the doctrine:

# Anti-AI-writing checklist · run on EVERY text

Banned vocabulary
- crucial, pivotal, revolutionary, essential,
  "in a rapidly evolving world", "it is important
  to note", "let us not forget that"

Banned structures
- the systematic rule of 3 (3 reasons, 3 steps, 3 benefits)
- the summary conclusion that repeats what was just said
- the meta-intro ("in this article, we will see…")
- bold on the first words of every paragraph
- decorative bullets that replace prose

Style
- no decorative metaphor: describe the fact, literally
- short sentences alternating with longer ones
- the WHY before the how
- one clear position per text, owned

If a box fails → rework the passage BEFORE delivery.

The checklist v1. You add to it every time you spot an AI tic in an output: it grows from one mission to the next.

The rule of 80. The writing standard up to 100 is not something you reach with AI alone. The agent takes you to 80, cleanly, in your voice. The last 20 are your own reading pass. That is non-negotiable.

And when you install the agent at a client's: you redo this step with THEIR voice. Their corpus, their rules, same method. That is what makes the system reusable without handing over your style.

Chapter 7

A skill does one precise thing. A workflow chains several skills to deliver a complete result: the output of one skill feeds the next. My system fits into four workflows, calibrated on dozens of projects: the audit, the keywords, the writing, the tracking. That is the rhythm I apply at my clients': the first week lays the foundation, then one workflow per week. After a month, the full cycle has run once.

And every published page has its evidence sheet, measured at day 30 and day 90: that is the output → learning loop from chapter 8. Quality control

This is the checkpoint where content is verified before it goes out. It fits into four criteria, and each is checked with a yes or a no:

QUALITY CONTROL · 4 criteria, all mandatory
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 [ ] 1. Every claim with a number has its source
        (zero "to be sourced" left in the published text)
 [ ] 2. The anti-AI-writing checklist passes
 [ ] 3. The page answers the intent targeted in the brief
        (not the keyword: the INTENT)
 [ ] 4. The page offers a concrete action
        (form, calculator, quote, meeting)

 otherwise → back to writing, with the failed criterion

A page that fails one criterion does not go out. Even one produced at 3 in the morning by a routine.

You do not create an article one article at a time. You create semantic coherence for the client. The agent holds that coherence across hundreds of pages, which no human holds by hand: that is precisely why the check has to be written down, not kept in your head.

My rule. While you are starting out, keep your skills isolated and chain them by hand. You only turn a chain into an automation once it has run without a hitch 5 times in a row. Not before. Automating a method that is not stable yet means freezing a mistake and replaying it forever.
Chapter 8

The four loops

This is the part everyone skips, and it is the one that separates an agent that produces from an agent that improves. A system that captures and produces without ever re-reading itself gets bigger, it does not get better.

The agent's 4 closed loops
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 LOOP 1 · capture → processing
 raw/ fills up ──→ weekly sort ──→ backlog listed ──→ ingestion
                                          │
 LOOP 2 · doctrine → validation           │
 hypotheses ──→ tested against sources ───┘
      ▲              │
      │              ▼ statuses: open / under test /
      │                validated / invalidated
      │
 LOOP 3 · output → learning
 page published ──→ evidence sheet + dated prediction
      │                    │
      │              D+30 / D+90: Search Console decides
      │                    │
      └──── the verdict corrects the doctrine ◄────┘

 LOOP 4 · correction → hardening
 you correct ──→ register ──→ 2nd time on the same theme
      ▲                              │
      └── the rule enters doctrine ◄──┘ (approved on Friday)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 Without loop 3, "my method works" stays a sales argument.
 Without loop 4, you redo the same corrections on every
 engagement. With them, you have facts and rules.

The four loops and what they have in common: everything ends up correcting the doctrine. That is the opposite of a system that piles up.

Loop 1 · capture → processing

Your raw/ folder fills up faster than it gets digested. The loop: once a week, the agent compares what is in raw/ with what has been ingested into wiki/sources/, and writes the backlog list into wiki/ingest-backlog.md, sorted by priority (field data first, industry watch after). You decide what to ingest, it ingests.

Loop 2 · doctrine → validation

Your doctrine holds convictions. Some are proven, others are bets. Two registers keep them honest: wiki/hypotheses.md (every unproven conviction, with its status) and wiki/contradictions.md (two pages of the vault saying the opposite, spotted at ingestion). Once a month, the agent tests the hypotheses against the sources ingested since the last review and moves the statuses forward.

Hard rule. A hypothesis never moves to "validated" on a gut feeling. It moves to validated on an evidence sheet backed by real data. Otherwise you build a doctrine that looks solid and is nothing but stacked opinion.

Loop 3 · output → learning

The most profitable of the four. Every published piece has to come back measured. The mechanism fits in one file, the evidence sheet, created at publication:

# wiki/evidence/2026-06-10-page-calculator-x.md

## The bet
- Page: /calculator-x
- Target keyword: "calculator x price"  (decision stage)
- Hypothesis tested: "a tool page captures more emails
  than an editorial page on the same query"
  → wiki/hypotheses.md #12

## Dated predictions
- D+30: the page gets its first impressions on the target
  query, position ≤ 20
- D+90: ≥ 1 lead attributable to the page

## Measurements (filled by the loop, never by hand)
- D+30 [date]: real position, impressions, clicks → verdict
- D+90 [date]: leads, position → verdict

## Final verdict
- hypothesis #12: confirmed / disproved / inconclusive
- what we take from it for the doctrine:

The complete evidence sheet. The D+30 and D+90 measurements come from a Search Console export: dropped into the vault by hand, or fetched through the connection in chapter 9 (service account + script). The agent fills the sheet from the export, never from its head.

After a few cycles, the agent no longer decides only with your doctrine: it decides with your doctrine corrected by your real results. SEO is a set of mathematical tasks repeated until patterns show up. This loop is what finds the patterns for you.

Loop 4 · correction → hardening

The most recent one at my end, and the one that pays for the other three. When you correct the agent (a title reworded, a banned word recalled, a format straightened out), the correction lives in the conversation and dies with it. Three weeks later, you make the same one again. The loop: every correction gets written into a register, wiki/recurring-corrections.md, with a stable theme slug. An isolated occurrence triggers nothing. The second occurrence of the same theme flips the status to "recurring", and the correction is proposed for hardening at the Friday review: a rule in the doctrine, a constraint in the skill concerned, or an automatic check that blocks delivery.

# wiki/recurring-corrections.md

| date  | theme            | correction              | status    |
|-------|------------------|-------------------------|-----------|
| 06-12 | question-titles  | no title as a question  | isolated  |
| 06-19 | question-titles  | (2nd time, same theme)  | recurring |
| 07-02 | invented-volume  | figure with no source   | isolated  |

recurring → proposed for hardening on Friday:
the rule enters the doctrine or the skill concerned,
and the mistake disappears for good.

The stable theme slug is the detection key: it is what lets you spot the 2nd occurrence. A hardened correction never comes back.

Hard rule. The agent never writes the rule itself into the doctrine or into a skill. It prepares the proposal, you approve it at the ritual. And when possible, prefer a mechanical check over a repeated instruction: a control that blocks delivery beats a guideline the model can forget. But you are the one who sets the lock.

The loop applied to production

The concrete case, the one I use on everything that gets published: you wrap the writing workflow in a loop. Every page produced feeds the next. That is the difference between writing 100 pages and writing the same page 100 times.

The production loop · each page learns from the last one
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
          ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
          ▼                                             │
 1. BRIEFING        the agent re-reads project memory:  │
          │         what worked, the rules learned      │
          ▼                                             │
 2. PRODUCTION      the writing workflow, in your voice │
          │                                             │
          ▼                                             │
 3. VERIFICATION    every claim with a number is        │
          │         checked: 2 sources, including the   │
          │         original source. Facts are kept     │
          │         separate from interpretations       │
          ▼                                             │
 4. CONTROL         the 4 criteria from chapter 7.      │
          │         It passes, or it does not go out    │
          ▼                                             │
 5. PUBLICATION     + the dated prediction (D+30/D+90)  │
          │                                             │
          ▼                                             │

          │                                             │
          ▼                                             │

          │         become lasting rules ───────────────┘
          ▼
   the next page starts higher than the last one

Steps 1 to 5, the agent runs on its own. Step 6 comes from the Search Console export (dropped by hand, or automated by the connection in chapter 9). Step 7 rewrites the project memory: that is what closes the loop. The 100th page is better than the 1st, not because the model changed, because the memory filled up.

What holds the loop together. A claim only enters a page once verified. A hypothesis is only validated by a measurement. A correction does not stay a correction, it becomes a rule. Remove one of those three locks and the loop still turns, but it learns your mistakes instead of your wins.

The ritual: 15 minutes on Friday

The loops produce proposals. Someone has to decide, and that someone is you. A weekly meeting, fixed agenda:

Weekly review · agenda (prepared by the agent)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 1. Evidence that came back this week (D+30 / D+90)
    → verdicts to approve
 2. Hypotheses that moved
    → promotions / invalidations to decide
 3. Open contradictions
    → which one to close this week
 4. Ingest backlog
    → next week's batch
 5. The recurring correction of the week
    → harden it into a rule (doctrine, skill or check)
 6. ONE direction decision (no more)

The agent proposes 95%, you arbitrate the 5% of irreducible judgment. If the ritual takes you an hour, your loops are badly tuned.

Chapter 9

Routines

Everything above can be launched by hand. Routines are what launch it for you, at fixed times. That is what turns the assistant into an agent.

The principle: Claude with no interface

Claude Code can run with no screen, with an instruction passed as an argument. That is the basis of every routine:

# The base command: Claude runs and stops
claude -p "Read AGENTS.md then sort the backlog:
compare raw/ and wiki/sources/, update
wiki/ingest-backlog.md, save."

The -p option launches Claude with no interface: it reads the instruction, works in the folder, hands control back. Everything a routine does goes through it.

Chapter 10

Managing your agent

At this point, the agent knows your rules, it is fed with your data, it runs your skills in your voice, it works at fixed times and it learns from its results. Your role changes: you no longer execute, you manage.

Holding your positions

If Claude states "this is how it is done in SEO" and your experience says the opposite, you are the one who is right. You rein it in, you give it the context again, you correct the doctrine. Be the manager, be sure of your positions. The day you approve without reading, the system drifts and you do not see it.

Turning every correction into a rule

You reword a title once, that is proofreading. You reword it three times for the same reason, that is a missing rule. The reflex to build:

The correction → lasting rule cycle
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 1. You correct an output                (proofreading)
 2. Same correction a 2nd time?           (signal)
 3. You write the rule:
    · writing tic      → anti-ia-writing.md
    · method mistake   → the SKILL.md concerned
    · general principle → AGENTS.md
 4. The mistake disappears FOR GOOD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 That is the only moment the doctrine is allowed to
 grow: never by anticipation, always by a correction
 you actually lived.

Doctrine and skills grow through use, not through theory. That is what keeps them applied.

Approve at the gates, not at every step

You are not there to re-read every paragraph. You are there at three moments: the brief before the writing, the quality control before publishing, and the Friday ritual. Three checkpoints, the rest is delegated. If you catch yourself micro-managing a step, the skill for that step is badly written: fix the skill, not the output.

Dosing the models

The big model for what needs judgment: strategy, briefs, arbitrating conflicts, quality control. Cheaper models for volume: variations, rewordings, mechanical tasks. That is your first place to save money once usage grows, and it is set per routine (each routine declares its model).

The real subject. You no longer pay for a single volume tool. Not on principle: because volume never brought you a client. What brings you clients is your data and your method, and the agent runs them at a scale you could not hold alone.
Chapter 11

The non-negotiable rules

Seven rules never to break. Keep this page open as long as your agent runs.

Loops 1 and 2 pay off from the second week (the backlog and the hypotheses fill up fast). Loop 3 has an unavoidable delay: the first evidence sheets return their verdict at day 30. That is the rhythm of SEO, not of the agent. You create the sheets from the first publication and you let Search Console decide.

"Can I install this at my clients'?"

Yes, and it is a model that changes the relationship: you are no longer a supplier delivering documents, you install a system the client keeps. Their voice, their data, your skills and your method.
